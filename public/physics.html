Model & limitations

This is a real-time, physically motivated visualization. It integrates Schwarzschild spatial null trajectories and combines them with a spin-dependent emitting disk. It is not a full Kerr spacetime or a magnetohydrodynamic simulation.

Optical model

Coordinates use rₛ = 2GM/c² = 1. Each screen pixel starts a ray in a local static observer frame. Its radial spatial tangent is multiplied by √(1 − 1/r) to initialize an affine tangent. The integrator advances

d²x/dλ² = −(3/2) h² x / r⁵,   h² = |x × dx/dλ|².

This is the vector form of the Schwarzschild orbit equation u″ + u = 3u²/2. An explicit midpoint method with a radius-dependent step approximates its solution. AUTO, HIGH and ULTRA permit 320 steps; MEDIUM 240; LOW 160 with longer steps. In black-hole mode, rays crossing the numerical boundary r = 1.001 terminate without boundary emission. Escaping rays sample the procedural celestial field using their asymptotic direction. Disk crossings contribute emission along the same trajectory.

The horizon is r = 1. The photon sphere is r = 1.5. The distant critical impact parameter is 3√3/2 ≈ 2.598. The shadow is not a sphere drawn in front of the scene. A captured ray can still carry foreground disk emission.

White hole / WH—02: corrected interpretation

A white hole is not a black hole with its color inverted. It is a past-horizon region in the maximally extended Schwarzschild geometry. Future-directed causal trajectories may emerge from that region. The exterior metric remains attractive and identical to the corresponding black-hole exterior; an image alone cannot determine the global causal extension.

Factory settings enable prescribed outgoing radiation and an illustrative emitting sheet for visibility. Both can be disabled for an unilluminated boundary-condition view. With all outgoing emission disabled, viewing rays whose past endpoint reaches the inner numerical boundary return zero radiance, while escaping rays see lensed stars. This is a boundary-condition experiment, not an exact interior solution. The visible dark region is not proof of a future black-hole horizon.

“Illuminate hypothetical outflow” explicitly enables a chosen source: uniform observer-normalized radiance at r = 1.001, an outward sheet beginning at 1.2 rₛ with illustrative radial β = 0.35, and optically thin exterior filaments. None of the color, luminosity, duration, energy budget or outflow geometry follows uniquely from the white-hole solution. The static metric does not respond to the injected energy. The model does not describe white-hole formation or stability. Classical white holes are vulnerable to infalling perturbations; Eardley’s analysis gives an important example of this instability.

Outgoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates u = t − r*/c, with r* = r + rₛ ln|r/rₛ − 1|, are regular at the past horizon. The causal formula panel uses this chart to distinguish the time orientation. Outgoing path markers reverse integrated subcritical spatial null orbits; their affine animation is not coordinate travel time. A UI crossfade is not a physical conversion between black and white holes.

Main-sequence stars / ST—03

Mass ranges from 0.1 to 40 solar masses. Temperature and radius are log-interpolated between selected monotone anchors from Eric Mamajek’s 2022.04.16 mean dwarf sequence, with an explicit solar anchor of 5772 K and 1 R☉. Selection of anchors avoids treating scatter in neighboring empirical mass estimates as a physical temperature inversion. The nearest tabulated effective temperature supplies an approximate spectral label. This is a representative sequence at roughly solar composition, not a unique mass-to-property law or stellar-evolution calculation.

Luminosity is derived self-consistently from L = 4πR²σT⁴. Surface gravity, escape speed, mean density, compactness and wavelength peak use the displayed mass, radius and temperature. Lifetime is only the rough scaling 10¹⁰ yr (M/M☉)/(L/L☉); changes in fuel fraction, mixing, composition and mass loss make it particularly approximate for fully convective and massive stars. The application neither evolves age nor solves the displayed interior equations.

Color comes from numerical integration of Planck radiance over 380–780 nm against analytic CIE 1931 color-matching fits by Wyman, Sloan & Shirley, then a linear sRGB conversion. Display radiance is normalized to show detail across the enormous luminosity range, followed by exposure and tone mapping. This is not a stellar-atmosphere spectrum or a literal brightness comparison. The Sun is approximately white in this view. Real late-M spectra contain molecular features omitted by a blackbody.

Radius is rendered in solar-radius units. Mass presets reframe the camera; the continuous mass slider holds the view unless avoiding the photosphere requires retreat. Rotation, granulation and spots are procedural, with cool-star patterns smoothly reduced for hot stars. Linear limb darkening approximates the observed center-to-limb gradient. Enhanced outer-atmosphere emission is artistic and adjustable, not a prediction of coronal luminosity. Ordinary main-sequence compactness is small, so stellar viewing rays are straight; no black-hole lensing is applied. Rapid-rotation oblateness, radiative winds, oscillations, binaries and MHD are not modeled.

Fundamental formulas

The optional panel computes geometric scales and static-observer quantities live from mass M and Schwarzschild areal radius r. It uses G = 6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², c = 299792458 m/s, M☉ = 1.98847 × 10³⁰ kg, ℏ = 1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, and kB = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K.

rₛ = 2GM/c²; rph = 1.5rₛ; bc = 3√3 GM/c²; A = 4πrₛ²; tₛ = rₛ/c. At a static exterior observer, dτ/dt = √(1 − rₛ/r), |dr/dt| = c(1 − rₛ/r) for radial null rays in Schwarzschild coordinates, and K = 12rₛ²/r⁶. The local measured speed of light remains c. Surface gravity κ = c⁴/(4GM) is the positive Schwarzschild magnitude normalized at infinity. The black-hole Hawking temperature ℏc³/(8πGMkB) is a nonrotating semiclassical benchmark, not the luminous disk temperature; it is not assigned to the hypothetical white-hole source.

Spin & disk

The prograde Kerr ISCO formula sets the nominal inner edge: rISCO/rₛ = [3 + Z₂ − √((3 − Z₁)(3 + Z₁ + 2Z₂))]/2, where Z₁ = 1 + (1 − a²)1/3[(1 + a)1/3 + (1 − a)1/3] and Z₂ = √(3a² + Z₁²). The implemented edge is clamped at 1.55 rₛ. The orbit-rate dependence is proportional to 1/(r3/2 + a/23/2).

Spin changes the disk edge and advection rate, but not the horizon or ray metric. There is no frame dragging, Kerr shadow displacement, or Kerr photon region. The nonzero-spin setting is an explicitly hybrid visualization.

Procedural density is advected differentially. Several noise scales, radial filaments, and a smooth outer taper supply structure. This is a thin, two-sided emitting sheet, with a residual transmission of 0.16 in the main body of the disk and smoothly tapered opacity at its edges to preserve secondary images. It has no fluid dynamics, disk thickness, self-gravity, or radiation pressure.

Frequency & brightness

The emission temperature falls approximately as r−0.72. A compact warm-to-white RGB palette approximates a blackbody appearance; it is not a Planck spectrum integrated through a detector response. The local orbital speed estimate is β = √[1/(2(r − 1))], capped for the hybrid inner disk. The frequency factor combines √[(1 − 1/r_emitter)/(1 − 1/r_observer)] with a special-relativistic Doppler factor. This includes the static observer lapse. Emission intensity is multiplied by g³ and the color temperature by g.

The Doppler direction is evaluated in the embedded spatial coordinates, not a complete emitter tetrad. Retardation of emission time is not modeled. Static camera gravitational blueshift is included, but moving-observer aberration is not. The spectral treatment is therefore qualitative, especially for close observers and nonzero spin. “Physical” keeps the unexaggerated prescription; “Observational” enables g → g1.65 to make frequency effects easier to see. Exposure, display tone mapping and the highlight shoulder are visual controls.

Scale, time & trajectories

Observer radius is a Schwarzschild coordinate radius, not a radial proper distance. Mass sets rₛ in kilometers, while the dimensionless image remains scale invariant. Simulation time is an illustrative advection clock, not elapsed physical seconds; the displayed speed is relative to that clock. No physical travel time or speed of a spacecraft is claimed.

Light Paths displays three separately integrated representative trajectories launched from a distant plane. Their moving markers advance in affine parameter. These lines are a spatial teaching overlay projected with the camera, not visible photon trails, not a second lensed observation of those lines, and not a measurement of coordinate light speed.

Numerical & artistic limits

  • The finite integration budget does not resolve arbitrarily high-order photon subrings. Near-critical trajectories and coarse quality can exhibit aliasing.
  • Stars are procedural point populations, not a catalog. The same ray calculation bends their apparent directions.
  • Disk temperatures and brightness are selected for readable warm emission. Real astrophysical disks can radiate predominantly outside visible wavelengths.
  • There is no volumetric scattering (the white-hole volume only emits), ray-bundle integration, temporal antialiasing, or rigorous bolometric transfer.
  • Auto quality adjusts GPU render resolution. WebGL 2 is preferred; unsupported environments use a clearly labelled CPU software preview with reduced resolution and noise detail. Software FPS can be much lower.
  • Audio is a quiet synthesized instrumental atmosphere and starts muted. It is not a sound recording of a black hole.

Optional bipolar plasma jets

The JET toggle adds an optically thin exterior plasma emissivity aligned with the disk axis. A widening collimated sheath, a weaker spine, helical brightness modulation and advecting knots are sampled along the same curved viewing rays as the disk. The emission begins outside 2.4 rₛ and tapers off between 38 and 55 rₛ. This finite display domain is not a physical jet termination.

The local illustrative velocity β controls δ = [γ(1 − β cos θ)]⁻¹. For an assumed synchrotron spectral index α = 0.7, a continuous-jet brightness prescription δ^(2 + α) gives view-dependent asymmetry; extreme brightness is capped for display. Gravitational frequency loss uses the existing approximate prescription. Knot motion uses the illustrative animation clock, not a calibrated coordinate velocity.

Jet color, collimation profile, normalization and magnetic texture are selected for visibility. No self-consistent magnetic field, particle acceleration, mass loading, spin-energy extraction, polarization, light-travel delay or GRMHD evolution is solved. A black-hole jet is exterior plasma, not particles escaping through the event horizon. White-hole jets are explicitly hypothetical. Mature main-sequence stars are not assigned these black-hole-style jets.

INTERSTELLAR / BH—01 + WH—02 · Model notes, September 2026

Ellis traversable wormhole / ER—04

This additional theoretical model uses ds² = −c²dt² + dℓ² + (ℓ²+a²)dΩ². Signed proper distance ℓ runs continuously between two asymptotically flat regions. The throat has minimum areal radius a, no event horizon and an unstable circular null orbit.

The renderer integrates ℓ′ = p, p′ = b²ℓ/(ℓ²+a²)², φ′ = b/(ℓ²+a²) with fourth-order Runge–Kutta and adaptive step size. It maps escaped rays to independent procedural celestial skies; higher-order winding near the critical orbit is limited by the finite integration budget. GPU integration uses up to 420 steps; the compatibility renderer interpolates a radial lookup, reintegrating branch transitions. No luminous throat rim or tunnel-wall texture is imposed.

The structure view is the equatorial spatial embedding R = √(ℓ²+a²), z = a asinh(ℓ/a). The moving ring identifies the observer's ℓ coordinate, not a physical shell. Camera motion is prescribed; camera-speed aberration is not included. Traversal is classical motion through a hypothetical spacetime, not quantum tunnelling. The Ellis model requires stress-energy violating the null energy condition. Existence, creation and stability are not established or simulated. Its ultrastatic lapse produces no gravitational frequency shift.

References: James, von Tunzelmann, Franklin & Thorne, Visualizing Interstellar's Wormhole (2015); Mishra & Chakraborty, On trajectories of null and timelike geodesics in different wormhole geometries (2018). This implementation uses the simpler Ellis metric, not the full cinematic geometry of James et al.

Stellar color, granulation and scale

The solar calibration is 5772 K: viewed without Earth's atmosphere, the integrated solar spectrum is approximately white, not saturated yellow. A blackbody spectrum integrated against approximate CIE color-matching functions supplies a temperature-dependent RGB color. Hue-preserving luminance compression avoids washing cool dwarfs and blue-white stars to identical white. This is a display approximation, not an atmosphere spectrum or an absolute radiance instrument. The optional enhanced-temperature palette increases color contrast and is explicitly labelled nonliteral.

Procedural fine cells, intergranular lanes, differential rotation and smaller spots provide the photospheric texture; they are illustrative rather than magnetohydrodynamic predictions. Changing mass compares separate main-sequence equilibrium stars, not an evolution animation. A mass, field-of-view or viewport change fits the physical radius into the smaller angular dimension; manual close approaches remain available. The scale bar reports projected kilometres at the stellar centre plane, not a geodesic distance on the curved surface. The world radius varies physically even when automatic framing keeps the disk a similar screen size.

Solar color reference: NASA Heliopedia.

Illumination, orientation and folded-space teaching views

White-hole factory settings now enable prescribed outward radiation so that the selected object is visibly identifiable. This is an illustrative source boundary, not a NASA observation or unique predicted appearance. The unilluminated solution remains selectable. NASA's Ask an Astrophysicist describes white holes as highly hypothetical.

Source orientation is an independent rigid rotation Rz(roll) Rx(tilt). Black-hole and white-hole rays transform into the source frame, and escaped directions return to the background frame. Disk and jets stay aligned; changing screen orientation does not invent a jet escaping the horizon. Stellar surface patterns rotate independently of the camera. In spherical Ellis geometry there is no distinguished physical spin axis; its orientation controls the background presentation and spatial diagrams.

Photon paths are RK4-integrated Ellis null geodesics with b/a = 0.55, 0.998 and 1.18: crossing, near-critical winding and reflection. They are drawn on the metric embedding, or in a labelled inset beside the optical view. They are teaching trajectories, not visible beams floating in vacuum.

Folded Space is a separate topological analogy inspired by the familiar folded-sheet explanation, not a global isometric embedding or a literal external view of our three-dimensional universe. The outer sheets and curved fold are schematic; the throat locally follows a catenoid. Long and short routes compare connectivity; their animated markers are not a calibrated light-travel calculation. The geodesic inset retains the actual Ellis metric. See NASA Cosmicopia's folded-paper explanation.

Cinematic passage changes field of view continuously near the throat, adds a slight camera bank and slows the prescribed camera path. Switch it off for a fixed field of view and constant playback rate. No warp-speed particles, new force, or camera-speed relativistic aberration is implied. Photospheric refinements add supergranular modulation, facular contrast and spot penumbral texture; these remain procedural illustrations rather than resolved stellar atmosphere calculations.

Adaptive rendering and image quality

AUTO changes pixel sampling, preserving the same ray equations and integration budgets (320 Schwarzschild / 420 Ellis steps). It seeks native display resolution at a selectable 30, 60 or 90 FPS target. Optional asynchronous GPU timer queries guide adaptation; frame timing is used when unavailable. HIGH requests native pixels; ULTRA requests 1.25 times native pixels per axis, further adjusted by Render scale. Buffers have a 12-megapixel limit (16 MP in ULTRA) and the hardware dimension limit. These limits and current pixel size are displayed; target FPS is not guaranteed on every device.

The CPU compatibility path is separate, visibly labelled, and uses reduced procedural detail and its own adaptive resolution at approximately 12 FPS. High-quality GPU mode requires WebGL2 availability in the browser session. The Performance panel reports actual resolution and renderer status, rather than assuming a selected label equals full GPU quality.

Magnetars & pulsars

Both presets represent neutron stars. Magnetar activity and pulsar behavior can overlap. The visible field lines, moving charge markers, surface colors and radiation cones are enhanced scientific illustrations, not visible-light photographs.

Closed near-zone dipole curves obey r = L sin²θ. Magnetar twist is a prescribed azimuthal deformation, not a force-free plasma solution. Closed shells extend to 8.8 stellar radii, within the light cylinder throughout the permitted rotation-period range. The pulsar additionally shows polar segments of high-L dipole curves, truncated at 23 R, below the light cylinder. These are a local teaching representation, not a global open-field solution. Outer winds and current sheets are outside this model.

Physical period P gives angular frequency 2π/P and light-cylinder radius cP/(2π). Playback is separate. The pulse trace shows a normalized Gaussian beam response at the camera direction. Radio and high-energy emission regions differ; this simple two-cone model does not predict flux.

Mass and radius determine compactness, surface gravitational redshift and mean density. Static bolometric surface intensity uses g⁴, where g² = (1 − rₛ/R)/(1 − rₛ/r_observer). A fixed illustrative source normalization and adjustable exposure preserve readable detail. Surface light bending, atmosphere spectra and full relativistic pulse-profile transfer are not integrated. The kilometer scale refers to the stellar-center plane. Magnetic energy density uses B²/(2μ₀), with gauss converted to tesla.

Magnetars emphasize twisted closed bundles, prescribed current-corona emission and crustal hotspots; pulsars emphasize sparse closed fields, polar segments and steady source-frame beams that rotate past the observer. Their colors represent high-energy emission, not literal visible spectra. Magnetar fractures, source textures and current brightness are illustrative. The optional magnetar flare has prescribed bundle brightening, polar expansion and decay. It is not an MHD calculation or measured flare light curve.
